Does each addition get in?

The charter's rule, fixed before the stage: against the plain guide on the same tickets, an addition must improve at least two of trustworthy rate, quality score and rescues, and cost no more than 30% extra. Differences from P1 are in brackets.

{{rule_table}}
  • Pointed skills gets in under every reading, frozen or corrected, current rules or second reading. Its trust gain is real but small at two runs per cell: half a ticket to one ticket in eight. Its quality edge is 0.06 of a point on a five-point scale, which is inside what the judge varies by. I would not lean on the quality number. Judge
  • What it costs you: about 10% more tokens, about 20% more words to read per ticket (1,990 against 1,650), and 0.7 stops per ticket against 0.4, because it asks more. Beyond the wrong-request tickets, its extra stops were one out-of-scope question, one question on a long ticket and one request to merge. Script
  • The rule has a weak spot this stage exposed. With rescues at zero for the plain guide, an addition can only get in by improving both trust and quality, and quality barely moves between any of these. That is why the architecture brief is out despite the largest trust gain. I applied the rule as written; changing it is yours to decide.

What you can use today

  • Add two lines to whatever guide your agents get: after reading the ticket and the code, check the request against the project's written decisions, docs and tests, and stop and ask if it breaks one; before writing the report, put each claim beside the command that proved it. That is the whole of what got in.
  • Do not expect a skills library to be used on its own. If a skill matters, say when to use it. This matches the outside test your idea was checked against.
  • Skip "small slices" as a rule for agents. It cost time and trust here.
