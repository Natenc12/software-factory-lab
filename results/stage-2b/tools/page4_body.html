What this means for a process you can carry anywhere

  • The portable part is enough. Six generic skills and two lines saying when to use two of them did as well as a map written for this codebase. Nothing in them names this project.
  • A map is optional, not harmful. Where a project already has one, adding it costs about 9% and does not get in the way. It is not worth writing one just for the agent.
  • Not yet shown: that any of this holds on a second codebase. That is stage 4. A project with no written decisions at all would test whether check-the-ask still finds anything to check against.

What to run next

The remaining questions are which model should build, whether a lead directing workers earns its cost, and whether any of this travels.

Why the charter's order no longer fits

The charter's rule says Fable builds only if it gains at least one trustworthy pull request in eight, or needs at least 30% fewer correction rounds. Opus with pointed skills now stands at 7.3 of 8, so the most Fable could gain here is 0.7, and Opus needed one correction round in 24 runs, so there is nothing to cut by 30%. On these tickets the rule cannot come out for Fable.

That is a finding in itself: with a decent ticket, the short guide and two pointing lines, the cheaper model is close to the ceiling of this test.

What I recommend

Fold stages 3 and 4 together. Pick one or two open-source projects in a different language or size, as the charter already plans for stage 4. Write and audit eight tickets for each, then run three setups there: pointed skills on Opus, the same on Fable, and your chief of staff (a Fable lead directing Opus workers as it sees fit).

Cost: about half a day of helper work to prepare each project, then 48 runs per project. I would bring you the candidate projects first, then a freeze note, as before.

  • Price, stated now as the charter requires: at list price Fable costs twice what Opus does per token.
  • If you would rather see the chief of staff sooner, the second option runs it here in about two hours, with the limits above.
