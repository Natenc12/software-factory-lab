# Index the first 250,000 words of an over-long upload instead of refusing it

## Problem

Students who upload a whole textbook get nothing. Any file with more than `MAX_INGEST_WORDS`
(250,000, in `src/gct/config.py`) is failed with reason `too_long` before anything is embedded, and
`GET /files/{file_id}` tells the student to split the file and upload the parts. Most of them don't:
they see "failed" and give up on the class.

## Why it matters

A partly indexed textbook is far more useful than a refused one. The first 250,000 words of a book
cover most of a semester.

## Scope

**In**
- Ingest (`src/gct/ingest/pipeline.py`, and the worker in `src/gct/jobs/worker.py` as needed).
- The status read and its message (`src/gct/files.py`, `src/gct/api/routers/files.py`).
- A new migration, if you need to store anything.
- Tests.

**Out**
- The value of the limit (it stays 250,000) and how words are counted (whitespace-separated words over
  the parsed text, as today).
- Chunking, the embedding provider, retrieval and the answer path.
- The web client, and the applied migrations under `migrations/` (byte-identical).

## Decisions

- A file with more than 250,000 words is no longer failed. Its first 250,000 words, in page or slide
  order, are chunked, embedded and indexed, and the file is published `ready`.
- Nothing past the first 250,000 words is embedded.
- For such a file, `GET /files/{file_id}` says that only part of it was indexed, and names both the
  limit and the file's own word count, written with thousands separators. For example: "Only the
  first 250,000 of this file's 312,400 words were indexed."
- Files within the limit behave exactly as today, and so does every other failure reason.

## Done when

- [ ] A 312,400-word upload ends `ready`, with chunks covering its first 250,000 words, and its status
      message names `312,400` and `250,000`.
- [ ] Files within the limit are unchanged.
- [ ] Tests for the above; every test passes; `uv run ruff check` and `uv run ruff format --check .`
      are clean.

## Worked examples

| # | Upload | After the worker has processed it | `GET /files/{file_id}` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A deck with 312,400 words | `ready`; chunks cover exactly its first 250,000 words; nothing after word 250,000 was embedded | `status` `ready`; `message` names `312,400` and `250,000`, e.g. "Only the first 250,000 of this file's 312,400 words were indexed." |
| 2 | A deck with exactly 250,000 words | `ready`; every word indexed | The usual ready message |
| 3 | A deck with 100 words | `ready` | `"This file is ready. You can ask questions about it now."` |
| 4 | A corrupt PDF | `failed`, `unparseable` | Unchanged from today |

## Failure cases that must hold

- A file of 250,001 words: the message names `250,001` and `250,000`.
- Retrieval over the class never returns text from past word 250,000 of an over-long file.

## Must not change

- `MAX_INGEST_WORDS` and how words are counted.
- Every other failure reason and its message, and the messages for `queued`, `processing` and a
  normal `ready` file.
- The applied migrations under `migrations/`.
- Existing tests: do not delete them or weaken their assertions.

## Acceptance tests

`tests/acceptance/test_over_long_uploads.py` is already in the branch. It encodes worked example 3
and the status message of worked example 1, and must pass. It does not cover everything above: the
rest of this ticket still applies.
